Privacy and Contact-Form Data

This page explains the website contact flow that can be verified in the current code and identifies details that should not be assumed.

Last updated: 14 August 2026

Draft governance note: management/legal review recommended before deployment. Operational terms should be confirmed by authorised management.

Information you choose to submit

The website contact form asks for your name, email address, subject, and message. Those fields help identify who is contacting the business, where a reply can be sent, and what the enquiry concerns. Avoid placing passwords, payment credentials, identity documents, private student records, or other unnecessary sensitive information in the message field.

You decide what to include. If your enquiry later requires documents or additional details, ask which information is needed and which channel should be used before sending it. The presence of a contact form does not mean every kind of confidential material is appropriate for that form.

How the message is routed

When the configured EmailJS settings are available, the form can send the name, email, subject, and message through EmailJS to the business. When those settings are not available, the website prepares a message for the visitor's own mail client. The visitor must use that mail application to complete the send.

EmailJS and a visitor's email provider are third-party services with their own terms and privacy practices. Links to maps, social networks, videos, or other external services also lead to sites controlled by their respective providers. Review a provider's policy before using it if its handling affects your decision.

Storage, security, and your choices

This page does not state a fixed retention period because one has not been verified in the site configuration. It also does not claim absolute security or that no third party processes a message. Internet and email transmission carry risk, so submit only what is needed for the enquiry and request clarification before sharing sensitive material.

You can contact the business to ask about a message you submitted or to raise a privacy concern. Include enough context to identify the message, but do not resend the sensitive information you are questioning. Operational answers should be confirmed directly rather than inferred from a general website statement.
